\title{SABER: Support-Conditioned Adaptive Bellman Error Reweighting for Sparse HER Actor-Critic}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{abstract}
Sparse goal-conditioned reinforcement learning commonly uses Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) to convert failed trajectories into relabeled Bellman targets. HER increases target quantity, but it does not decide which relabeled Bellman residuals are most useful for fitting a critic near the current policy action. We call this the hindsight Bellman critic-fit usefulness gap.
We propose SABER, a support-conditioned adaptive Bellman error reweighting objective. For each HER sample, SABER checks whether the current policy action lies inside a local replay-action support set for the same state-goal-horizon context, then applies a larger batch-normalized weight only to the critic Bellman residual. HER relabeling, replay sampling, actor objective, critic architecture, and backbone target construction are unchanged.
On FetchPickAndPlace, SABER improves one-update-per-step matched HER baselines across DDPG, TD3, and SAC in mean final success and success AUC, with statistically clearer AUC gains on TD3 and SAC. DrawerOpen DDPG provides a large-effect case, while an HGR-style sampler-only row and random/PER controls prevent a canonical goal-prioritization or unique-causality claim. High replay-ratio HER and REDQ-HER can match or exceed SABER's environment-step AUC under larger update/backbone budgets, and DDPG/TD3/SAC diagnostics support mixed critic error allocation rather than a lower-noise reliability proof.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sparse-reward goal-conditioned RL is dominated by the question of how to obtain useful training signal when successes are rare. HER addresses this by relabeling failed trajectories with achieved goals~\citep{andrychowicz2017her}, turning failures into additional goal-conditioned Bellman targets. However, HER chooses \emph{which goals} to train on; it does not decide whether every relabeled Bellman residual is equally useful for fitting the critic near the action currently queried by the actor.

This distinction matters in off-policy actor-critic. The critic regresses Bellman targets at replay actions, while the actor improves by querying the critic at current policy actions. If the current policy action lies outside the local replay-action support for the same state-goal context, critic estimates in the actor-query region may be more fragile. We do not claim that this is a theorem or that support-positive targets are intrinsically lower-noise. Instead, we study local replay-action support as a policy-relevant proxy for allocating critic fitting pressure after HER.

SABER is a narrow intervention: it reweights only the critic Bellman loss. It does not change HER relabeling, replay sampling, actor objective, backbone Bellman targets, or critic architecture. The paper is therefore a matched-backbone study of post-HER critic-loss allocation under the standard one-update-per-step regime, with high replay-ratio HER and REDQ-HER as compute/backbone-scaling references.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate the hindsight Bellman critic-fit usefulness gap: relabeled target quantity and current-policy critic supervision quality are distinct.
    \item We introduce SABER, a support-conditioned critic-only Bellman residual reweighting objective that can be attached to DDPG, TD3, and SAC HER backbones.
    \item We provide FPP cross-backbone evidence, DrawerOpen large-effect evidence, random/PER/scaling controls, an HGR-style sampler-only ablation, and diagnostics that define a cautious mixed-mechanism boundary.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Scope}

HER and later goal-conditioned methods improve sparse reward learning by changing how training goals, episodes, transitions, or targets are produced~\citep{andrychowicz2017her,ren2019hgg,fang2019cher,zhao2018ebp,luu2021hgr,yang2021mher}. Canonical HGR combines TD-error episode/goal sampling with importance correction; our current DrawerOpen row keeps only the sampler and is labeled accordingly. ARCHER rescales hindsight rewards, while USHER uses an importance ratio to correct HER Bellman bias~\citep{lanka2018archer,schramm2023usher}. SABER instead keeps rewards, replay sampling, and one-step targets fixed and applies a local-support allocation weight without an unbiasedness claim.

PER prioritizes large TD-error samples~\citep{schaul2015per}. SABER does not rank by TD-error magnitude; its signal is local support of the current policy action. We nevertheless include PER-style and diagnostic comparisons because support-positive samples can be enriched among high-error samples. REDQ and sparse-goal REDQ-HER use high update-to-data ratios and Q ensembles~\citep{chen2021redq,hiraoka2023sparsegoalredq}; these are important compute/backbone-scaled references but are outside SABER's matched-backbone regime. The actor-critic backbones are DDPG, TD3, and SAC~\citep{lillicrap2015ddpg,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac}. Experiments use Fetch and Meta-World manipulation tasks~\citep{plappert2018multigoal,yu2020metaworld}.

\section{Method}

For a replay transition \(i\), let \(x_i=(s_i,g_i,h_i)\) denote a local state-goal-horizon context, \(a_i^r\) the replay action, and \(a_i^\pi=\pi(s_i,g_i)\) the current policy action. SABER builds a local pool \(L_i\) of replay actions with similar context and discretizes actions into bins. Let \(B_i\) be the set of occupied action bins in this local pool. The support indicator is
\begin{align}
\psi_i={}&\mathbb{1}\{|L_i|\ge N_{\mathrm{local}}\}
\mathbb{1}\{\min(|L_i|,M_B)\ge M_{\mathrm{avail}}\}\notag\\
&\cdot \mathbb{1}\{b_i^\pi\in B_i\}.
\end{align}
This predicate is a coarse local-support proxy, not a direct target-noise estimator.

SABER maps \(\psi_i\) to a raw weight \(\omega_i=1+\lambda_S\psi_i\), then batch-normalizes it:
\begin{equation}
\Omega_i=\frac{\omega_i}{\frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}\omega_j}.
\end{equation}
For a one-step HER Bellman target \(y_i\) and critic \(Q_\theta\), the critic objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_Q(\theta)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal B|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}\Omega_i\left(Q_\theta(s_i,a_i^r,g_i)-y_i\right)^2.
\end{equation}
For TD3 and SAC, the same \(\Omega_i\) is applied to each critic's Bellman residual. Actor updates and target networks remain exactly those of the underlying backbone.

\section{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate sparse FetchPickAndPlace (FPP), FetchSlide, and Meta-World DrawerOpen/DrawerClose wrappers. Main Fetch rows use 1M environment steps, seeds \(1,\ldots,5\), HER ratio 0.8, batch size 256, and 20 evaluation episodes. FPP SAC AUC and Hit90 use the exact shared grid \(\{50\mathrm{k},\ldots,1\mathrm{M}\}\) with no interpolation or filling; this removes the legacy 25k versus newer 10k evaluation-frequency mismatch. Drawer rows use 2M steps. Steps/sec and runtime are observed, not hardware-normalized claims.

The central comparison is matched-backbone one-update-per-step HER versus HER+SABER. Controls include an HGR-style sampler-only row, PER-style/full PER, random-support, shuffled-weight, random-fixed-count, high replay-ratio HER, REDQ-HER target-Q-bound, and sample-level diagnostics. Canonical HGR and HGG/MHER/CHER are not directly compared in this version.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

SABER's contribution is not that it beats the strongest compute-scaled HER variants. Its value is to isolate a simple, attachable, critic-side allocation point after HER: which relabeled Bellman residuals should exert more fitting pressure while the rest of the HER actor-critic system remains fixed. This makes SABER useful both as a method and as a diagnostic lens for understanding when support-conditioned critic fitting differs from random reweighting, PER-style weighting, update scaling, and backbone scaling.

The limitations are substantial. First, the support predicate is task-specific and uses coarse membership, not a density/diversity guarantee. Second, diagnostics do not prove lower-noise targets. Third, support lookup has nontrivial runtime overhead. Fourth, the current HGR-style row omits canonical importance correction, while HGG, MHER, and CHER remain untested. Fifth, results use five seeds and several stress-test CIs are wide.

\paragraph{Diagnostic evidence.}
Instrumented DDPG, TD3, and SAC runs log 500 full diagnostic batches and 64k sample rows per backbone. Support-positive rates are 0.367, 0.219, and 0.197, while Pearson correlations with \(|\delta|\) are only 0.116, 0.128, and 0.143. Their top-10\% TD-error overlaps are 0.516, 0.360, and 0.342, respectively: enriched over each support base rate but far from TD-error equivalence. TD3/SAC support-positive samples also receive larger immediate residual correction after normalizing by weight. Target variance is higher, not lower, for support-positive samples in all three diagnostics.

\paragraph{Statistical reporting.}
Primary comparisons use the same public seeds 1--5 and report sample standard deviation. Paired deltas use 10,000 seed bootstrap resamples; final success and Hit90 are reported alongside AUC rather than used to claim universal significance. Native SAC curves remain archived, while all cross-method SAC AUC, final, and Hit90 values are recomputed only at exact shared 50k checkpoints without interpolation or forward filling.

\section{Reproducibility and Anonymity}

The blind package includes a hashed FPP SAC common-grid artifact with 70 inputs, exact-grid code, seven tests, per-seed/aggregate/paired outputs, and a pinned NumPy dependency. Native metrics remain unchanged and are retained for audit. The DrawerOpen sampler-only package contains five seed summaries and configs but is not labeled canonical HGR. Public repository URLs, user namespaces, and raw commit hashes are omitted; observed throughput is not a normalized hardware benchmark.
