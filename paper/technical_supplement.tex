\section{Experimental and Reproducibility Details}

\subsection{Tasks, Cohorts, and Shared Training Settings}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
This section fixes the experimental scope before any method is compared. Its
purpose is to identify which quantities are paired, which quantities are shared,
and which task--backbone combinations support a matched SABER claim.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Tables~\ref{tab:supp-task-cohorts}--\ref{tab:supp-backbone-settings} give the
settings shared within each matched cohort. The primary comparison contains
seven task--backbone cohorts and uses paired seeds 1--5. Drawer SAC is not a
primary cohort because a complete matched HER--SABER pair was not collected.

\input{tables/supplementary_experimental_settings}

\paragraph{Tuning boundary.}
We did not conduct an exhaustive joint hyperparameter search. The Main SABER
settings were fixed for the matched comparisons, and no control received
variant-specific tuning. The \(K\in\{3,7\}\) runs are sensitivity checks around
Main \(K=5\), not a per-task model-selection sweep.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
Within each paired cohort, HER and SABER share the task, seed, training budget,
replay and relabeling code, backbone, optimizer, exploration process, UTD, and
evaluation grid. Their intended algorithmic difference is therefore the
per-sample critic-loss coefficient. Absolute AUC values are not compared across
tasks because horizons and training budgets differ.

\subsection{Comparison Methods}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The comparison suite separates three operational intervention points after
experience collection: hindsight selection, replay-transition selection, and
fixed-minibatch critic-loss allocation.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
\input{tables/supplementary_comparison_settings}

\paragraph{Prioritization controls.}
HGR prioritizes an episode and then a transition--future-goal pair, multiplies
the two normalized importance factors on relabeled samples, and leaves the
original-goal coefficient at one. PER samples replay transitions with
TD-error priority and annealed importance correction. Both change realized
selection, unlike SABER and the TD-error critic-loss weighting control.

\paragraph{TD-error critic-loss weighting.}
This control preserves uniform replay and changes only normalized per-sample
critic coefficients. DDPG uses its single absolute TD error. SAC uses
\(\min(|\delta_{i1}|,|\delta_{i2}|)\) as the signal and applies the resulting
coefficient to both critic losses. For signal \(e_i\), the implementation
computes
\[
p_i=(e_i+10^{-6})^{0.6},\qquad
u_i=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{p_i}{|B|^{-1}\sum_{j\in B}p_j},\,0,\,2\right),\qquad
\Omega_i=\frac{u_i}{\max(10^{-8},\,|B|^{-1}\sum_{j\in B}u_j)}.
\]
Thus the uncapped priorities are mean-normalized before clipping and the
clipped coefficients are normalized again to unit minibatch mean. TD3 results
are not reported because the stored control used independent target-policy
smoothing noise for weight
construction and optimized residuals, preventing the intended matched-signal
interpretation.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
HER is the direct matched baseline. HGR and PER are broader prioritization
comparisons because they alter realized selection, whereas TD-error weighting
is the closest alternative allocation-signal control where validated. Thus an
ordering against HGR or PER does not isolate loss allocation alone, while an
ordering against TD-error weighting does not establish superiority over every
possible weighting signal.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Analysis}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The metrics distinguish full-training performance, late-training performance,
final-checkpoint behavior, and threshold-reaching time without smoothing away
seed variability.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Let \(q_j\in[0,1]\) be deterministic evaluation success at exact shared
checkpoint \(t_j\). Normalized success AUC is
\[
\operatorname{AUC}
=\frac{1}{t_m-t_1}
\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}
\frac{q_j+q_{j+1}}{2}(t_{j+1}-t_j).
\]
Final-quarter AUC applies the same calculation to 750k--1M for FPP and
1.5M--2M for Drawer tasks. Final success is the value at the final checkpoint.
Hit90 is the first exact checkpoint with \(q_j\geq0.9\); its median is
computed only over seeds that reach the threshold, so reached-seed coverage
is always reported.

\paragraph{Checkpoint alignment and unit of inference.}
For seed $s$, checkpoint success is the mean of 20 binary outcomes from
deterministic-policy evaluation episodes. Before computing a comparison, we
restrict every included method to the intersection of its available checkpoint
labels; AUC, FQ-AUC, and final success for that comparison use this same grid.
The statistical unit is the seed rather than an episode or checkpoint. Thus a
paired comparison first forms one difference $d_s$ per shared seed, and the
paired bootstrap resamples the vector of seed-indexed differences. Evaluation
episodes and checkpoints are never treated as independent replicates. A seed
that does not reach success $0.9$ contributes to reached-seed coverage but is
not assigned an artificial Hit90 time.

\paragraph{Evaluation isolation.}
Evaluation uses the deterministic policy with exploration disabled and a
seed stream separate from training. Evaluation transitions are not inserted
into replay and trigger no optimizer or target-network update. Checkpoint
labels count training environment interactions only, so evaluation frequency
does not change the stated training budget or replay contents.

\paragraph{Cohort separation.}
Primary claims use the paired five-seed cohorts only. The frozen FPP TD3
ten-seed cohort, indexing sensitivity, and Drawer diagnostic seeds 6--10 are
reported as confirmatory, sensitivity, or diagnostic extensions and are not
silently pooled into primary confidence intervals. Where a pooled ten-seed
diagnostic summary is shown, it is labeled descriptive.

For paired comparisons, each seed contributes one method difference. Primary
and newly derived intervals use 10,000 paired percentile-bootstrap resamples
with pseudorandom seed 2027. The frozen mechanism-control intervals reproduce
their validated source artifacts: FPP uses 10,000 resamples with seed 27027,
whereas Drawer controls use exact empirical-bootstrap enumeration over all
\(5^5=3125\) paired bootstrap assignments.
Sample standard deviations use \(n-1\) in the denominator. No curve is
smoothed, interpolated, extrapolated, or forward-filled.

\paragraph{Metric and uncertainty boundary.}
The trapezoidal AUC weights each interval by elapsed environment steps, so a
denser checkpoint schedule does not receive extra mass merely by containing
more checkpoints. Normalized AUC and FQ-AUC remain in \([0,1]\). Method-wise
mean and sample SD describe across-seed level and dispersion, whereas paired
intervals quantify uncertainty in within-seed differences. An interval that
includes zero is treated as inconclusive rather than as evidence of
equivalence. Hit90 uses only an observed checkpoint crossing and is never
interpolated between evaluations.

\paragraph{AUC boundary handling.}
The full-span integral begins at the first shared recorded checkpoint \(t_1\)
and ends at \(t_m\); we do not prepend a synthetic zero-step success value.
FQ-AUC restricts the same checkpoint sequence to the stated task-specific
window and normalizes by that window's observed elapsed span. The exact grids
contain the window endpoints, so neither metric requires a fractional or
interpolated checkpoint. Final success is \(q_m\) on the same shared grid.
These rules are applied unchanged to every method in a reported comparison.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
Where reported, Steps/s is the seed-1--5 mean of completed training steps
divided by runtime. Because historical runs were not hardware-normalized across
families, it is descriptive rather than a learning-efficiency claim. Control
and reference tables use a common metric order; diagnostic tables retain
signal-specific columns.

Hit90 tests whether a method reaches a practically high success level and how
early it first does so, complementing metrics that average over checkpoints.

\input{tables/supplementary_primary_hit90_table}

\section{Additional Controls and Sensitivities}

\subsection{Mechanism Controls}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The four DDPG controls test the query action, sample--query alignment, and the
location of high critic weights while exposing the absolute performance
metrics underlying the paired AUC differences in the main paper.

\input{tables/supplementary_mechanism_controls_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
SABER Main has higher observed mean full-span AUC than every control on FPP,
DrawerOpen, and DrawerClose. The Main-minus-control interval excludes zero
for FPP Random-Support and DrawerOpen Replay-Action Query; the other ten
intervals include zero. Late metrics are less uniform: for example,
Random-Support and Context-Eligible Count-Matched have higher mean FQ-AUC than
Main on DrawerClose.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The controls show that query identity and weight placement can affect learning
and that SABER's AUC ordering is not explained solely by the number of high
weights. Most five-seed intervals remain inconclusive, however, and the
absolute metrics show that a full-span AUC direction need not extend to every
late or final-checkpoint measure.

\subsection{Allocation Signal}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
This control asks whether SABER benefits merely from assigning unequal critic
coefficients, or whether current-policy local support provides a different
allocation signal from TD error. Both methods retain uniform replay and change
only normalized critic-loss coefficients.

\input{tables/supplementary_td_error_weighting_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
SABER has higher observed mean AUC in all four validated cohorts, but every
paired 95\% interval includes zero. Late metrics are mixed, including higher
FQ-AUC for TD-error weighting on DrawerClose.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The common mean direction distinguishes SABER from this specific TD-error
allocation control, but the five-seed intervals do not establish a conclusive
per-cohort advantage. TD3 is omitted because its stored control does not
support the intended matched-signal comparison.

\subsection{Action-Bin Resolution}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
This sensitivity tests whether the Main action discretization \(K=5\) is
robust to coarser and finer bins and records how bin resolution changes the
frequency of support activation.

\input{tables/supplementary_action_bin_sensitivity_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
Main \(K=5\) has higher mean AUC than both \(K=3\) and \(K=7\), with both

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The evaluated \(K=5\) setting is favored over both adjacent resolutions in

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Actor and Critic Weight Scope}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
This ablation tests whether the support coefficient should modify the critic,
the actor, or both. All variants retain the support query, support
hyperparameters, and minibatch normalization. Critic-Only weights Bellman
losses; Actor-Only weights per-sample actor terms; Actor+Critic weights both.

\input{tables/supplementary_actor_scope_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
Critic-Only has the highest observed mean AUC in each of the five completed
Main-versus-scope comparisons. Main-minus-variant differences range from
\(+0.017\) to \(+0.141\), but every 95\% interval includes zero. DrawerOpen
Actor-Only also has substantial seed dispersion
\((0.612\pm0.343)\). FPP Actor+Critic remains pending and is not inferred.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The available results are consistent with using critic-only weighting as the
conservative main design, while providing no decisive evidence that actor
weighting is intrinsically harmful. Each scope changes the subsequent
training trajectory, so realized support masks need not remain identical.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{No-HER Replay References}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
AER is included because it also exploits similarity between the agent's
current interaction context and stored replay states, but uses that signal to
change minibatch membership. SABER instead preserves the sampled HER
minibatch and changes only critic-loss allocation. The comparison separates
uniform experience replay (ER), uniform replay with AER's actor/critic
learning-rate schedule (Annealing ER), and Attentive Experience Replay (AER).
AER ranks an enlarged uniform candidate set by cosine similarity to the
current online state and trains on the 256 most similar transitions. The three
arms therefore test attentive selection beyond its shared schedule and whether
replay selection alone can solve these sparse tasks.

\input{tables/supplementary_aer_noher_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
Without HER, FPP AER/Annealing ER/ER obtain mean AUCs of
\(0.044/0.129/0.148\) with DDPG, \(0.039/0.039/0.039\) with TD3, and
\(0.040/0.041/0.064\) with SAC. On DrawerOpen, the corresponding values are
\(0.000/0.111/0.579\) with DDPG and \(0.000/0.000/0.089\) with TD3; on
DrawerClose they are \(0.194/0.199/0.150\) and \(0.169/0.176/0.251\).
AER has no higher observed mean AUC than Annealing ER in these seven
transferred cohorts.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Across these no-HER transfers, state-similarity replay does not improve
observed mean AUC over its schedule-matched uniform-replay control. The
three-arm design isolates attentive selection beyond AER's annealed schedule.
Without a fixed-learning-rate AER arm, however, it does not identify a
schedule-by-selection interaction. This is not a general replication or
rejection of AER; it isolates transferred AER components under the present
tasks and backbones.

\subsection{Update-Budget and Architecture References}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The FPP SAC High-RR comparison changes HER's update-to-data ratio from one to
four while retaining its two-critic SAC architecture. REDQ-HER is a broader
architecture-and-compute reference evaluated on FPP, DrawerOpen, and
DrawerClose: it uses UTD 4, ten critics, a random two-critic target subset,
and target-Q bounding. REDQ-HER TQB clips the sampled target-critic backup to
the sparse-reward range \([-50,0]\) before forming the Bellman target.

\input{tables/supplementary_high_rr_table}
\input{tables/supplementary_redq_references_table}
\FloatBarrier

\paragraph{Results.}
On FPP SAC, increasing HER from UTD 1 to UTD 4 raises observed mean AUC from
\(0.471\) to \(0.768\). REDQ-HER records AUC \(0.867\) on FPP, \(0.672\) on

target construction, and entropy optimization. These results therefore
provide scaling context rather than matched one-factor tests of SABER.

\section{Support-Signal Diagnostic Details}

\subsection{Instrumentation and Aggregation}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The diagnostics test whether SABER's support rule activates in practice,
whether it is redundant with TD-error ranking, and whether the resulting
weights alter immediate critic fitting in the intended direction.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Diagnostic logging introduces no gradient term and does not change replay
sampling, objectives, or parameter-update order. FPP logs one full minibatch
every 10k environment steps for DDPG, TD3, and SAC seeds 1--5: 100 batches
per run, 500 per backbone, and 1,500 total. Drawer diagnostics use a
200-step cadence from 5k through 2M, producing 9,976 persisted snapshots per
seed. Snapshot quantities are aggregated within seed before cohort
statistics are computed.

For each persisted minibatch, the diagnostic stores the pre-update absolute
Bellman residual, support membership, and the residual after one critic
update. The fixed-target residual is
\[
\Delta|\delta_i|
=\left|Q_{\phi}^{\mathrm{pre}}(x_i,a_i^r)-y_i^{\mathrm{pre}}\right|
-\left|Q_{\phi}^{\mathrm{post}}(x_i,a_i^r)-y_i^{\mathrm{pre}}\right|.
\]
Thus the target and minibatch are held fixed while the critic parameters
change. Live-target quantities, where available, are retained only for audit
and are not mixed with the fixed-target statistic.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
These measurements audit activation and one-update optimization behavior on
persisted minibatches. They do not by themselves identify the independent
cause of a long-horizon return difference.

\subsection{Cohort-Level Results}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The cohort summary jointly measures activation frequency, association with
pre-update error, and support-conditioned residual reduction under a common
fixed-target definition.

\input{tables/supplementary_support_signal_diagnostics_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
Primary-cohort support-positive rates range from \(0.197\) to \(0.417\), so
the indicator is neither nearly absent nor nearly constant. Primary Pearson
correlations are modest (\(0.084\)--\(0.143\)), while exact top-decile
enrichment ranges from \(1.379\times\) to \(1.828\times\). The
support-positive/support-negative one-step residual-reduction ratio ranges
from \(1.79\times\) to \(3.94\times\) in the primary cohorts. Both Drawer
extensions preserve enrichment above one and a larger reduction for
support-positive samples. The support-positive mean reduction is larger in
all 25 primary backbone--seed runs.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Support-positive samples are enriched among high-error samples, but the weak
linear correlations show that support does not reduce to a TD-error ranking.
The one-step statistic verifies that the normalized coefficients redistribute
immediate critic fitting as intended; it is not a standalone explanation of
long-term learning performance.

\subsection{Phase-Conditioned Allocation}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The phase analysis asks where support activation occurs during Drawer
progression and whether it merely follows the most frequent phase.

\paragraph{Phase definitions.}
Let \(d_h\) be the Euclidean hand--handle distance and \(d_g\) the
handle--relabeled-goal distance. Open/Closed denotes \(d_g\leq0.05\).
Opening/Closing denotes \(d_g>0.05\), \(d_h\leq0.08\), and
\(d_g\leq0.12\). Contact denotes \(d_h\leq0.08\) and \(d_g>0.12\);
Approach contains the remaining samples. These mutually exclusive rules
reproduce the implementation's terminal-to-approach assignment precedence.

\input{tables/supplementary_phase_diagnostics_table}
\input{tables/supplementary_phase_seed_counts_table}

\paragraph{Results.}
In both Drawer tasks and in both primary and extension cohorts, approach
and contact have higher support-positive rates than the dominant terminal
phase, even though the terminal phase accounts for roughly three quarters
of logged samples. Table~\ref{tab:supp-phase-seed-counts} reports the exact
phase counts and support-positive rate for each primary seed; the accompanying
artifact also contains exact support-positive counts and seeds 6--10 rows.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Activation therefore does not simply track phase frequency. The task-specific
phase labels localize the signal but do not constitute an independent causal
test of long-horizon performance.

\input{tables/supplementary_runtime_environment_table}
\input{tables/supplementary_runtime_components_table}

\section{Artifact and Provenance Index}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
This index connects each reported experiment to a validation status and
repository-relative artifact location, so completed evidence is distinguishable
from references, extensions, and pending work.

\input{tables/supplementary_artifact_index_table}

\paragraph{Seed mapping.}
For paired seed \(s\), the environment, PyTorch, NumPy, and action-noise streams
use \(s\); the
support-weight stream uses \(20000+s\), and evaluation uses \(10000+s\).
Training and evaluation resets advance by deterministic episode- and
checkpoint-indexed offsets. All methods in a paired cohort use this mapping.

\paragraph{Interpretation boundary.}
Generated analysis directories contain compact CSV summaries, a
repository-relative source manifest, and a self-excluding artifact manifest.
Canonical experiment artifacts retain configuration, environment snapshot,
source provenance, and validation records where available. Raw result files
are referenced rather than duplicated in the derived summaries. An indexed
artifact establishes traceability to the reported result; it does not convert
a secondary reference into a matched causal comparison.

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The seed-level appendix exposes every observation underlying the primary
aggregate table, allowing readers to inspect paired directions and
between-seed dispersion directly.

\paragraph{Results.}
SABER has higher observed mean AUC than matched HER in all seven primary
cohorts, and four paired 95\% intervals exclude zero. The individual rows show
that this aggregate statement coexists with negative or near-zero seed pairs
and especially large dispersion on the Drawer TD3 cohorts. AUC and
final-quarter AUC use each cohort's exact common grid.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
These rows are the observations used in the primary analysis, not an
additional independent experiment. They make the direction and uncertainty
of each paired cohort auditable without changing the seed as the unit of
statistical analysis.
